Spanda · logo exploration v2

Three new directions for the mark

The first mark (a dot with rings) read as a target or a Wi-Fi icon, so I dropped it. Each concept below starts from what Spanda means: a still thing that comes alive when you move. Click any large mark to replay its motion.

Concept A

The Kolam S

Recommended

A single unbroken line loops around two dots and becomes an S.

In South India, a kolam is drawn at the doorstep every morning: one continuous line weaving around a grid of dots. Here the dots are scenes and the line is the scroll that carries you from one to the next, in one continuous take. Western viewers see a clean S monogram. Anyone who knows kolam sees the tradition.

Why it works

  • It's the S of Spanda, so the mark and the name reinforce each other
  • The heritage is real but hidden, which is exactly the brief
  • The motion is built in: the line draws itself, then the scenes land
  • Holds up at 16px as a simple S with two dots

Watch out

  • Needs a careful final drawing so it never reads as a dollar sign (keep the dots inside the curves, never on a vertical stroke)

Concept B

Scene Planes

Three scenes stacked in depth, sliding toward you.

Every Spanda site is a sequence of scenes moving in 3D space as you scroll. The mark shows that directly: layered planes, back to front, the way parallax works.

Why it works

  • Instantly says "3D, depth, scenes"
  • Very simple to draw, animate and scale

Watch out

  • Close to the generic "layers" icon used by many design tools, so it's hard to own
  • No connection to the name or the Indian story

Concept C

Still to Wave

A still point, and a wave leaving it.

The most literal reading of the name: the dot is stillness, the wave is the pulse that sets it in motion. It works like a tagline you can see: "Stillness, set in motion."

Why it works

  • Tells the name's meaning at a glance
  • Works well as an animated loader inside the app

Watch out

  • Wave lines are common in audio, health and fintech logos and can read as a heartbeat
  • Wide shape, so it fills a square app icon awkwardly
