The Kolam S as a Mandana

Mandana is folk art from Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh: patterns drawn in white chalk (khadiya) on walls and floors coated in red-ochre clay (geru). Here the S is turmeric yellow and the lamps are chalk white, on a red-ochre square with a dotted chalk border. Shown next to the current mark, Neel with gold dots, in the same places.

What the Mandana version does well

  • Rooted in a real art form, with a story you can tell in one line
  • The warmest, most eye-catching icon in a dock full of blue and gray apps
  • Handmade, artistic feel that suits a tool for artistic websites
  • Contrast passes: yellow on red-ochre 4.1:1, white on it 6.8:1

Where it works against the brief

  • Red plus yellow is the most overtly "Indian" palette. For a Western-first audience it reads as a theme, not a hidden touch, and it's close to the saffron-and-gold look we chose to avoid
  • Red and yellow together also suggest fast food and fuel brands in the West
  • It stops matching the studio, whose accent is Neel, so the app icon and the app inside feel like two brands
  • On a dark website header it needs its red square every time; it can't sit as a bare line
Recommendation

Keep the current mark as the main logo. Use the Mandana version as a special edition.

The Mandana version is beautiful, but it's loud for a Western-first pro tool and it splits the app from its icon. It works well where heritage should be on show: the About page, India launch material, festival editions (Diwali, Holi), merchandise and stickers. That keeps the main brand calm and consistent, while giving the Mandana art a proud, deliberate place.
